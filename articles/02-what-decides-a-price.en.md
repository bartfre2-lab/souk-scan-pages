# What decides a price? Why potatoes never cost the same all year

## One product, many prices

Potatoes are in almost every Moroccan kitchen. Yet the price of a kilo is never fixed. It can be different from one month to the next, and even from one stall to another on the same day.

This is not a mystery. Many things push a price up or down at the same time. Here are the main ones, using potatoes as the example.

## Quality and grade

Not all potatoes are the same. Variety, size, skin, cleanliness and freshness all differ. Nicola, Spunta and Désirée are among the varieties grown in Morocco.

Buyers usually judge quality with their eyes and hands. The Competition Council noted in 2024 that Morocco has no common official reference for grading fruit and vegetables. So a seller's "first quality" and another seller's "first quality" may not be the same thing. The Council also observed that, for each product, markets show a spread between a lowest and a highest price, which fits the range of qualities on offer.

## Where it comes from

Potatoes do not all grow in one place at one time. A potato crop takes only three to four months. According to the Council, early crops are planted in September to January along the coast, from Rabat-Salé and Casablanca to El Jadida and Souss-Massa. The main crop is planted in January and February. A late crop is planted in August and September, mostly in inland plains such as Tadla, Haouz, Saïs and Moulouya. In the mountain valleys of the Atlas, planting is mostly in May.

So the potatoes on sale change through the year, and so do their growing areas. The Council also reports that six regions produce, on average, 83% of Morocco's fruit and vegetables. Many products therefore travel a long way to reach the city.

## Season and weather

When a lot of a product arrives at the same time, prices tend to fall. When little arrives, prices tend to rise. The Council describes this seasonal pattern as a major feature of fruit and vegetable prices. In its analysis of prices for 2015 to 2022, the season explained about 56% of the movement in wholesale prices of dry onions.

Weather can change the picture quickly. In August 2023, Hespress reported that extreme heat had damaged vegetable crops, according to a professional source. In January and February 2026, floods in the north covered potato fields in the Loukkos basin, according to the market-monitoring platform EastFruit, as reported by Hespress. Weather surprises are hard to predict, so prices can jump when they happen.

## Transport and storage

Fresh produce is perishable. It must be harvested, packed, moved and sold before it spoils. Every step has a cost: trucks, crates, fuel, market fees, cold rooms.

Losses are part of the story. The Council reports estimates that between 20% and 40% of Moroccan fruit and vegetables are lost after harvest. A 2022 report by the UN Environment Programme and FAO says that a lack of effective refrigeration is one of the leading causes of food loss worldwide.

Only some vegetables can be stored for long. According to the Council, they are mainly onions and certain kinds of potatoes. Some stock is kept in advance to cover possible gaps in supply, for example during Ramadan.

## Demand and supply

The basic rule is simple. If there is more produce than buyers, prices tend to fall. If there is less produce than buyers want, prices tend to rise.

Demand also follows the calendar. The Council gives examples: people buy more summer fruit in summer, and more root vegetables such as carrots in winter. Demand is not always the same from one week to the next, which is one more reason prices move.

## Imports and exports

Morocco both sells and buys potatoes abroad, so events elsewhere can reach the local market. Morocco has exported potatoes to West African countries. In August 2023, Hespress reported that the government had extended its ban on exports of potatoes and onions to those countries. The stated aim was to keep the home market supplied.

Imports play a role too. EastFruit reported that between July 2025 and June 2026 Morocco imported more potatoes for eating than in recent years, mainly from the Netherlands, France and Belgium, after the floods hit local production. Some farmers also plant imported certified seed potatoes, according to the Council.

Each of these choices has trade-offs. Exports bring income to farmers. Imports and export limits aim to keep enough supply for households.

## Wholesale price and shop price

The **wholesale price** is what is paid for large lots, for example at a wholesale market. The **shop price** is what you pay per kilo at your corner shop or stall.

They are linked but not the same. The Council found that retail prices follow wholesale prices closely. For round tomatoes between 2015 and 2022, the two moved almost together. So when the wholesale price changes, the shop price usually follows.

But a shop also has its own costs and choices. The Council notes that retailers take into account their location, their customers, the conditions of sale and their running costs. And wholesale markets are not the only route: the Council estimates that only 30% to 40% of national production passes through them. The rest is sold through other channels. A wholesale price is a useful reference, not the whole picture.

## No single reason

A price is the result of all these things at once. That is why no price stays the same for long, and why a range tells you more than one number.
